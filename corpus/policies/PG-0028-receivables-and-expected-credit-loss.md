# Trade Receivables, Credit Control and Expected Credit Loss

This policy covers granting credit, chasing what is owed, providing for what probably will not
arrive, and writing off what definitely will not.

## 1. Credit assessment

No customer is invoiced on credit terms before a credit limit is set. Limits are set by the Group
Credit Manager on the basis of an external credit report, trading history, and where relevant,
financial statements.

| Requested limit | Approver |
|---|---|
| Up to USD 100,000 | Group Credit Manager |
| USD 100,001 to USD 500,000 | Finance Director |
| Above USD 500,000 | Chief Financial Officer |

Limits are reviewed annually and immediately on an adverse credit event. An order that would take a
customer above its limit is held; releasing it requires approval at the band for the revised total
exposure, not for the value of the order.

## 2. Terms

Terms are set centrally by customer segment. A sales team may not agree terms outside the standard
without Finance Director approval, and terms agreed in a contract that has not been reviewed by
Finance are not binding on the Group's credit process.

## 3. Collection

| Days past due | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Automated reminder | System |
| 14 | Telephone contact, outcome logged | Credit Control |
| 30 | Account placed on hold for new orders; account manager notified | Credit Control |
| 45 | Formal demand letter | Group Credit Manager |
| 60 | Escalation to Finance Director; consider third-party collection | Group Credit Manager |
| 90 | Legal action considered with Group Counsel | Finance Director |

Placing an account on hold is the Credit Manager's decision. Commercial pressure to release a hold is
escalated to the Finance Director rather than resolved between the sales team and Credit Control.

Disputed invoices are recorded as disputed with the reason and owner, and are excluded from the
ageing-based provision in §4 only where the dispute is genuine and documented. A dispute raised on
the day payment falls due is treated as a collection issue until substantiated.

## 4. Expected credit loss

The Group applies the simplified approach to trade receivables and contract assets, recognising
lifetime expected credit losses from initial recognition. A provision matrix is applied by ageing
band and customer segment:

| Days past due | Base provision rate |
|---|---|
| Not yet due | 0.5 per cent |
| 1 to 30 | 2 per cent |
| 31 to 60 | 5 per cent |
| 61 to 90 | 15 per cent |
| 91 to 180 | 40 per cent |
| Over 180 | 100 per cent |

Base rates are derived from at least three years of the Group's own loss history and are
recalibrated annually. They are then adjusted for forward-looking information — sector outlook,
macroeconomic indicators relevant to the customer base, and known events affecting a specific
customer. The adjustment and its basis are documented each period; applying the matrix unadjusted
because the adjustment is difficult is not acceptable.

Specific provisions are made in addition where a customer is in insolvency, has broken a payment
plan, or where recovery is otherwise doubtful, and they replace rather than supplement the matrix
rate for that balance.

Balances with government bodies and with other Group entities are excluded from the matrix and
assessed individually.

## 5. Write-off

A receivable is written off when there is no reasonable expectation of recovery: the customer is
dissolved, the claim is statute-barred, a settlement has been agreed for less than the balance, or
collection cost would exceed the amount recoverable.

**write-off of a receivable above USD 25,000** requires Finance Director approval, per PG-0007 §4.
Below that amount, the Group Credit Manager may approve. Every write-off records the reason, the
recovery steps taken, and whether the customer remains on the approved list.

Write-off is an accounting recognition, not a legal release. The Group continues to pursue written-off
balances where it is economic to do so, and recoveries are credited to the period in which they are
received rather than restated against the original period.

## 6. Credit notes

Credit notes are approved by someone other than the person who raised the original invoice. Credit
notes above USD 10,000 require Finance Director approval. A credit note issued to clear an aged
balance rather than to correct a genuine billing error is a write-off and follows §5 instead; the
distinction is tested each quarter by Internal Audit.

## 7. Reporting

Credit Control reports monthly: total receivables, ageing profile, days sales outstanding by segment,
accounts on hold, disputes by age and value, provision movement with the reason, and write-offs. The
report goes to the Finance Director and is reviewed at the close meeting under PG-0016.
