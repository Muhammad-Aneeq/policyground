# Revenue Recognition — Variable Consideration, Constraint and Financing

This policy covers step 3 of the framework in PG-0011: determining how much the Group expects to be
entitled to when the amount is not fixed.

## 1. What counts as variable

Any amount that could change based on future events: volume rebates, tiered pricing, performance
bonuses and penalties, service-level credits, price concessions, rights of return, refunds, and
milestone payments contingent on an outcome.

Consideration is variable even where the contract states a single price, if the Group's customary
business practice creates a valid expectation of a concession. A history of granting end-of-quarter
credits makes the price variable however the contract is worded.

## 2. Estimating the amount

Two methods, chosen by which better predicts the outcome and applied consistently to similar
contracts:

- **Expected value** — the probability-weighted amount across possible outcomes. Used where the
  Group has many similar contracts, such as a volume rebate scheme across a customer base.
- **Most likely amount** — the single most likely outcome. Used where there are few possible
  outcomes, such as a milestone bonus that is either earned in full or not at all.

The method is chosen once per contract type, documented, and not switched between periods to
produce a preferred result.

## 3. The constraint

An estimate of variable consideration is included in the transaction price only to the extent it is
highly probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue will not occur when the
uncertainty resolves. This is a deliberately cautious test.

Factors that increase the likelihood of reversal, and therefore reduce the amount included:

- the amount depends on factors outside the Group's influence, such as a customer's own end-market
  demand, weather, or the act of a third party;
- the uncertainty will not resolve for a long time;
- the Group has limited experience with similar contracts, or its experience has limited predictive
  value;
- the Group has a practice of offering concessions or changing terms;
- the contract has a broad range of possible outcomes.

Sales-based and usage-based royalties on a licence of intellectual property are an exception: they
are recognised only as the sales or usage occur, regardless of the estimate that could otherwise be
made.

## 4. Reassessment

Estimates are updated at each reporting date. A change is recognised as an adjustment to revenue in
the period of change, allocated on the same basis as at inception, except where the variable amount
relates entirely to one distinct obligation and allocating it there faithfully depicts the amount
the Group expects.

Reassessment is not optional and is not deferred to year end. Where a rebate accrual has been
materially wrong for two consecutive quarters, the estimation method itself is reviewed.

## 5. Rights of return

Revenue is recognised for the goods expected **not** to be returned. A refund liability is
recognised for the expected returns, and an asset is recognised for the right to recover those
goods, measured at former carrying amount less expected recovery cost. The asset is presented
separately from inventory and is assessed for impairment each period.

Return rates are estimated by product family using at least twelve months of history. A new product
with no history uses the rate of the closest comparable family, and the choice is documented.

## 6. Significant financing component

Where the timing of payment gives the customer or the Group a significant benefit of financing, the
transaction price is adjusted to the cash selling price and the difference is presented as interest.

The Group applies the practical expedient of not adjusting the transaction price where the period
between transfer of the goods or services and payment is one year or less. A financing component is
assessed, and presumed to exist until shown otherwise, whenever the **payment timing difference
exceeds 12 months**.

Not every long payment period creates a financing component. Where the payment schedule exists to
protect either party against non-performance, or where the amount varies for reasons other than the
passage of time, no financing component arises. The assessment and its conclusion are documented.

The discount rate used is the rate that would be reflected in a separate financing transaction
between the Group and the customer at inception, which reflects the customer's credit
characteristics. It is not the Group's own borrowing rate, and it is not revised for later changes.

## 7. Non-cash consideration

Consideration in a form other than cash is measured at fair value at contract inception. Where fair
value cannot be reasonably estimated, the standalone selling price of the goods promised is used
instead. Customer-supplied goods or services are consideration only where the Group obtains control
of them.

## 8. Consideration payable to a customer

Amounts paid or payable to a customer — slotting fees, marketing contributions, coupons — reduce
revenue unless they are in exchange for a distinct good or service the customer transfers to the
Group, at no more than its fair value. Any excess over fair value reduces revenue. Payments to a
customer's customer are treated the same way.
