# Capitalisation Thresholds

This policy states the monetary thresholds at which expenditure is capitalised rather than
expensed. It is the single reference point for thresholds; PG-0004 governs how a capitalised
asset is subsequently measured, and PG-0005 governs software specifically.

## 1. Principle

A threshold is an administrative convenience, not an accounting principle. Expenditure is
capitalised only if it first meets the recognition criteria — a resource controlled by the Group,
from which future economic benefit is expected — and then meets or exceeds the applicable
threshold below. Failing the recognition test, an item is expensed however large it is.

## 2. Thresholds

| Category | Threshold | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| General property, plant and equipment | USD 5,000 | Unit cost of the individual asset |
| IT end-user hardware | USD 1,000 | Unit cost |
| Leasehold improvements | USD 25,000 | Total cost of the fit-out project |
| Internally developed software | USD 10,000 | Total qualifying project cost |
| Low-value lease exemption | USD 5,000 | Value of the underlying asset when new |

In narrative terms:

- The **general capitalisation threshold of USD 5,000** applies to items of property, plant and
  equipment that do not fall into a more specific category below.
- The **IT equipment capitalisation threshold of USD 1,000** is deliberately lower than the general
  threshold. This is an asset-tracking control as much as an accounting rule: laptops, handsets and
  peripherals are recorded in the asset register so that custody is traceable, even though the
  amounts are individually small.
- The **leasehold improvement capitalisation threshold of USD 25,000** is applied to the fit-out
  project as a whole, not to each invoice within it.
- The **software capitalisation threshold of USD 10,000** applies to total qualifying development
  cost for a project, assessed under PG-0005.
- The **low-value lease threshold of USD 5,000** determines eligibility for the low-value lease
  exemption in PG-0027.

## 3. Applying a threshold to a unit, not an invoice

The threshold is applied to the smallest item that functions independently.

- Twelve chairs at USD 700 each on one invoice are twelve items of USD 700. They are expensed.
- A production line delivered as forty components that cannot operate separately is one asset.
  Its cost is the total of the components.
- A laptop and its docking station are one unit if the dock is issued with the laptop and has no
  independent use.

Splitting a single asset across invoices or purchase orders to stay below a threshold is a
control breach and is reported under PG-0007.

## 4. Costs included and excluded

Included: purchase price after trade discounts, non-recoverable duties, freight, installation,
site preparation, and professional fees directly attributable to bringing the asset into use.

Excluded: training, general administrative overhead, relocation of an existing asset, costs
incurred after the asset is capable of operating as intended, and any element of internal profit.

## 5. Repairs, replacements and improvements

Routine maintenance is expensed. Expenditure is capitalised only where it extends the useful life
of the asset, increases its capacity, or improves the quality of its output. Where a significant
component is replaced, the carrying amount of the component replaced is derecognised even if it
was not separately identified at acquisition. If its original cost is not known, the cost of the
replacement is used as an estimate of what the replaced component originally cost.

## 6. Depreciation of capitalised IT

For consistency with the lower IT threshold, **IT hardware is depreciated over 3 years**. Full
useful-life tables for other categories are in PG-0004.

## 7. Exceptions

An exception to any threshold in this policy requires Finance Director approval in advance, in
writing, with the reason recorded against the asset in the fixed-asset register. Retrospective
exceptions are not granted.
