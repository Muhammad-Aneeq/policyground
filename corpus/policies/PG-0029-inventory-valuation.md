# Inventory Valuation, Costing and Obsolescence

Inventory is carried at the lower of cost and net realisable value. Most errors in practice come
from what is included in cost, and from provisions that are set once and never revisited.

## 1. Cost formula

The Group uses weighted average cost for all inventory categories. First-in-first-out is permitted
only where a local statutory requirement demands it, and the difference to weighted average is
reported with the entity's submission. Last-in-first-out is not used anywhere in the Group.

The formula is applied consistently to all inventories of a similar nature and use. An entity does
not switch formula between periods.

## 2. What is included in cost

**Included:** purchase price after trade discounts and rebates, non-recoverable duties, inbound
freight, direct labour, and the systematic allocation of fixed and variable production overhead
based on normal capacity.

**Excluded:** abnormal waste, storage costs other than those necessary before a further production
stage, administrative overhead that does not contribute to bringing inventory to its present
location and condition, selling costs, and settlement discounts, which are treated as finance income.

Normal capacity is the production expected on average over a number of periods under normal
circumstances. Where actual production is abnormally low, the fixed overhead per unit is **not**
increased; the unallocated overhead is expensed in the period. This is the most common inventory
valuation error found in review, because it is the one that flatters gross margin in a bad quarter.

## 3. Net realisable value

Net realisable value is the estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business, less the
estimated costs of completion and the estimated costs necessary to make the sale.

It is assessed item by item, or by group where items are related and cannot be evaluated separately.
It is not assessed by comparing the total carrying amount of all inventory with total expected
revenue, which would allow losses on one line to be masked by margin on another.

Raw materials are not written down below cost where the finished goods they will become are expected
to sell at or above cost. Where they are not, replacement cost is the best available measure of net
realisable value for those materials.

## 4. Obsolescence provisioning

A base provision is applied by ageing band, computed from movement history:

| No movement for | Base provision |
|---|---|
| 6 to 12 months | 10 per cent |
| 12 to 18 months | 30 per cent |
| 18 to 24 months | 60 per cent |
| Over 24 months | 100 per cent |

The matrix is a starting point, not the answer. It is adjusted for known facts: a discontinued
product line is provided in full regardless of age; a slow-moving spare with a documented service
obligation may be provided at less than the matrix rate, with the reason recorded and the service
obligation referenced.

Provisions are reassessed each quarter. A provision released because stock has finally sold is
reported separately from the period's charge, so that the gross movement is visible.

## 5. Physical counts

Every location performs either a full count at least annually or a perpetual count cycle covering
every item at least once a year, with high-value items counted quarterly.

Counts are performed by people independent of day-to-day stock custody, or with an independent
observer present. Count sheets do not show book quantities. Differences above USD 10,000 by line, or
above USD 50,000 in aggregate for a location, are investigated before the ledger is adjusted, and
the investigation conclusion is recorded.

A location whose net count difference exceeds 2 per cent of inventory value in two consecutive counts
is placed under a documented remediation plan.

## 6. Consignment and third-party stock

Stock held by the Group on consignment from a supplier is not recognised until control passes. Group
stock held by a third party is recognised and is confirmed in writing by the holder at each half
year; a confirmation from the holder's sales contact is not sufficient where the amount exceeds USD
250,000.

## 7. Work in progress

Work in progress is valued at the stage of completion reached, using the standard routing for the
product. Where a job has stalled, its costs are reviewed for recoverability rather than accumulated
indefinitely; a job with no activity for two close cycles is reviewed with operations and written
down where completion is no longer expected.

## 8. Reporting

Each entity reports monthly: inventory by category, ageing profile, provision movement with reasons,
count differences, and the value of stock held by third parties. Unallocated production overhead
expensed under §2 is reported separately so that it is not confused with a provision movement.
