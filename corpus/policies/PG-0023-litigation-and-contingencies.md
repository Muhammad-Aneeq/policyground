# Litigation, Claims and Contingency Provisioning

**Distribution:** Chief Financial Officer, Group Counsel, Group Financial Controller, and the named
members of the litigation review panel. Working papers prepared under this policy are held under
legal privilege where privilege applies, and the privileged material is never attached to a journal
or stored in the general ledger system.

## 1. Why this policy is restricted

A provision figure discloses the Group's own assessment of its exposure. Circulating it to a wide
internal audience risks waiving privilege and risks the estimate reaching a counterparty, which
would prejudice settlement. The estimate must nevertheless be recorded accurately, so access is
narrow rather than the information being softened.

## 2. Recognition

A provision is recognised where all three hold:

1. there is a present obligation arising from a past event;
2. it is more likely than not that an outflow will be required;
3. the amount can be estimated reliably.

Where an outflow is possible but not probable, or the amount cannot be estimated reliably, no
provision is made and a contingent liability is considered for disclosure. Where an outflow is
remote, neither provision nor disclosure is required.

An obligation must arise from a past event that has already occurred. The Group's intention to
settle a claim, or its wish to avoid reputational damage, does not create a present obligation.

## 3. Measurement

The provision is the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the
reporting date.

- For a single obligation, the most likely outcome, adjusted where other outcomes are mostly higher
  or mostly lower.
- For a population of similar obligations, the expected value across the range.
- Where the effect of the time value of money is material and the timing is reasonably predictable,
  the provision is discounted at a pre-tax rate reflecting current market assessments and the risks
  specific to the liability. The unwinding of the discount is a finance cost.

Legal costs expected to be incurred in defending the claim are included in the provision where they
are unavoidable, whatever the outcome of the claim itself.

## 4. The quarterly review

Group Counsel maintains the litigation register. Every matter above the reporting threshold is
reviewed quarterly by the litigation review panel, which records for each matter: the stage reached,
external counsel's current assessment, the range of outcomes, the point estimate, the movement since
last quarter and its cause, and the expected timing.

Matters are referenced by their register code, never by counterparty name, in any document that
leaves the panel. The reference format is illustrated by the standing employment matter recorded as
**Matter Reference Kestrel-14**, which is the format all matters follow.

External counsel is asked for an assessment of the range of outcomes and their relative likelihood.
Counsel is not asked to select the accounting number; that determination is made by the Group and is
documented as the Group's own judgement.

## 5. Reimbursements

Where a third party — typically an insurer — is expected to reimburse some or all of the
expenditure, the reimbursement is recognised as a separate asset only where receipt is virtually
certain, and only up to the amount of the provision. The provision is presented gross; the
reimbursement asset is presented separately and is never netted against it.

"Virtually certain" means the insurer has confirmed cover for the specific matter. A policy that
appears to respond is not sufficient.

## 6. Changes and use

Provisions are reviewed at each reporting date and adjusted to the current best estimate. A provision
is used only for the expenditure for which it was originally recognised; applying a provision raised
for one matter against another is prohibited without exception, because it conceals both the release
and the new charge.

Where a provision is no longer required, it is released in the period the obligation ceases, with the
release explained in the flux commentary rather than absorbed into the underlying line.

## 7. Contingent assets

A contingent asset is never recognised. Where an inflow becomes virtually certain, the asset is no
longer contingent and is recognised at that point. Claims the Group is pursuing are disclosed only
where an inflow is probable, and the disclosure avoids any statement that would prejudice the claim.

## 8. Disclosure

Disclosure of a provision or contingency is drafted by Group Counsel with the Group Financial
Controller and is approved by the Audit Committee. Where disclosure would seriously prejudice the
Group's position in a dispute, the exemption from full disclosure is applied, the general nature is
disclosed, and the reason for the reduced disclosure is recorded in the Audit Committee minutes.
