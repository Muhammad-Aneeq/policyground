# Foreign Currency Transactions and Translation

This policy sets out which rate is used, where the resulting difference goes, and who supplies the
rates. Consistency matters more here than precision: an entity choosing its own rate source makes
the consolidation impossible to reconcile.

## 1. Functional currency

Each entity determines its functional currency by reference to the currency that mainly influences
its selling prices, its labour and material costs, and the currency in which it retains its
receipts. It is a matter of fact, not of choice, and it changes only when the underlying
circumstances change.

A change in functional currency is applied prospectively from the date of change and requires Group
Technical Accounting approval with the supporting analysis retained.

The Group presentation currency is USD.

## 2. Rate sources

Rates are published centrally by Treasury on the first working day of each month and are the only
rates entities may use. Entities do not source rates from their bank, their local ERP default, or a
public website.

| Rate | Use |
|---|---|
| Daily spot rate | Individual transactions above USD 250,000 equivalent |
| Monthly average rate | Income statement items, and transactions below USD 250,000 equivalent |
| Month-end closing rate | Monetary assets and liabilities at the period end |
| Historical rate | Non-monetary items measured at historical cost, and equity |

The monthly average is a simple average of daily rates, not a period-end approximation. Where a
currency has moved by more than 10 per cent within a month, Treasury issues a weighted average
instead and notifies entities with the reason.

## 3. Transactions in the entity's own books

A transaction is initially recorded at the rate ruling on the transaction date, per §2.

At each period end:

- **Monetary items** — cash, receivables, payables, loans — are retranslated at the closing rate,
  and the difference is recognised in profit or loss.
- **Non-monetary items at historical cost** — inventory, property, plant and equipment, prepayments
  for goods — are not retranslated.
- **Non-monetary items at fair value** are translated at the rate ruling when fair value was
  measured, and the difference follows the fair value gain or loss.

Exchange differences on trading balances are presented in operating expense. Differences on funding
balances and cash are presented in finance income or expense. The distinction is applied
consistently and is not used to reposition a loss below the operating line.

## 4. Translation of a foreign operation for consolidation

| Item | Rate |
|---|---|
| Assets and liabilities | Closing rate at the reporting date |
| Income and expense | Average rate for the period |
| Equity and pre-acquisition reserves | Historical rate at the date of the transaction |
| Goodwill and fair value adjustments arising on acquisition | Treated as assets of the foreign operation; closing rate |

The resulting difference is recognised in other comprehensive income and accumulated in the
translation reserve. It is recycled to profit or loss only on disposal of the operation, and only to
the extent of the interest disposed of.

## 5. Close mechanics

Entities submit in their functional currency. Translation is performed centrally by Group Reporting
during consolidation, after **group submission on Working Day 8**. Entities do not translate their
own submission into USD, because doing so hides the translation difference inside entity results.

Where an entity holds a balance in a currency other than its functional currency, that balance is
reported separately in the submission so that the retranslation in §3 can be verified.

## 6. Hedging

The Group hedges transactional exposure where a firm commitment exceeds USD 1,000,000 equivalent and
settlement is more than ninety days away. Hedging is executed by Treasury alone; entities do not
enter derivative contracts.

Net investment in a foreign operation is hedged only with Board approval. Hedge accounting is
applied only where documentation is in place at inception; where it is not, the derivative is
measured at fair value through profit or loss and the volatility is explained in the flux commentary
under PG-0016.

## 7. Highly inflationary economies

Where an entity operates in an economy assessed as highly inflationary, its results are restated
before translation, using an index published by Group Technical Accounting. Entities do not make
this assessment locally.
