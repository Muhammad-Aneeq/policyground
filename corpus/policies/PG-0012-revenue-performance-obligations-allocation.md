# Revenue Recognition — Performance Obligations and Price Allocation

This policy covers steps 2 and 4 of the framework in PG-0011: identifying what was promised, and
attaching the price to those promises.

## 1. Identifying distinct promises

A promised good or service is distinct, and therefore a separate performance obligation, where both
of the following hold:

- the customer can benefit from it on its own or with resources readily available to them; **and**
- the promise is separately identifiable from the other promises in the contract.

The second test is the one that is misapplied. A promise is *not* separately identifiable where the
Group is providing a significant integration service, where the item significantly modifies or
customises another item in the contract, or where the items are highly interdependent. In those
cases the contract contains one combined obligation, however many line items appear on the order
form.

## 2. Common arrangements

| Arrangement | Typical conclusion |
|---|---|
| Licence plus mandatory hosting the customer cannot operate without | One combined obligation, recognised over the hosting term |
| Licence plus optional support the customer could buy elsewhere | Two obligations |
| Equipment plus routine installation any competent contractor could perform | Two obligations |
| Equipment plus complex commissioning only the Group can perform | One obligation |
| Subscription plus onboarding with no standalone value to the customer | One obligation; onboarding fee spread over the subscription term |
| Product plus a warranty that only assures the product works as specified | One obligation; assurance warranty is a provision, not revenue |
| Product plus a warranty providing additional service beyond assurance | Two obligations |

An onboarding or set-up fee that the customer would not pay to anyone else is almost never a
separate obligation. It is a payment in advance for the main service.

## 3. Customer options and material rights

Where a contract gives the customer an option to acquire additional goods at a discount that would
not be available without the contract, the option is a separate performance obligation — a material
right — and part of the transaction price is allocated to it. Renewal options priced at the
prevailing rate do not create a material right.

Loyalty points and account credits granted on purchase are material rights. Revenue attached to
them is deferred until they are redeemed or expire unexercised.

## 4. Allocating the transaction price

The transaction price is allocated to each obligation in proportion to the standalone selling price
of each, determined at contract inception and not revised for subsequent changes in price.

The best evidence of standalone selling price is the observable price when the item is sold
separately. Where an item is never sold separately, the price is estimated using, in order of
preference:

1. an adjusted market assessment, based on what a customer in that market would pay;
2. expected cost plus the margin the Group would target for that item;
3. a residual approach — permitted **only** where the selling price is highly variable or
   uncertain, and never for more than one obligation in a contract.

Where observed standalone prices cluster, the Group applies a **standalone selling price tolerance
band of 15 per cent** around the median observed price. Contracts pricing an item within that band
are treated as consistent with the standalone price and no reallocation is made. Contracts pricing
outside the band are reallocated and the reason is documented.

## 5. Discounts

A discount is allocated proportionately across all obligations unless there is observable evidence
that it relates to only some of them — which requires that the Group regularly sells those items
separately, and regularly sells them at that discount as a bundle. Sales-side assertions about
which item "the discount was really on" are not observable evidence.

## 6. Contract modifications

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Additional distinct goods at their standalone selling price | Separate new contract |
| Additional distinct goods, not at standalone selling price | Terminate and replace: remaining consideration spread over remaining obligations prospectively |
| Change to goods that are not distinct from those already partly delivered | Cumulative catch-up adjustment to revenue |

The treatment is determined by the substance of the change, not by whether the paperwork is a new
order or an amendment.

## 7. Documentation

Every contract above USD 250,000 in total value has a revenue memorandum on file before revenue is
first recognised, recording: the obligations identified, the standalone selling prices used and
their source, the allocation, the timing conclusion for each obligation, and who approved it.
Memoranda are reviewed by Group Technical Accounting on a sample basis each quarter.
