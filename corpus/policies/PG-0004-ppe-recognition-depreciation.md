# Property, Plant and Equipment — Recognition, Depreciation and Disposal

This policy governs the measurement of tangible fixed assets after the capitalisation decision has
been made under PG-0003.

## 1. Recognition

An item is recognised as property, plant and equipment when the Group controls it, expects to use
it for more than one reporting period, expects future economic benefit from it, and can measure
its cost reliably. The applicable monetary thresholds are set in PG-0003 and are not restated here
in full; the **general capitalisation threshold of USD 5,000**, the **IT equipment capitalisation
threshold of USD 1,000** and the **leasehold improvement capitalisation threshold of USD 25,000**
are the three that arise most often.

## 2. Measurement at recognition

Assets are measured at cost. The Group does not apply the revaluation model to any class of
property, plant and equipment. Assets acquired in a business combination are measured at fair
value at the acquisition date and then carried at that amount less accumulated depreciation.

Assets received as a gift or at nil consideration are recorded at fair value with the credit
recognised in other income, unless the transfer is from another Group entity, in which case the
carrying amount transfers unchanged.

## 3. Useful lives

Depreciation is charged on a straight-line basis from the month the asset is available for use.

| Class | Useful life | Residual value assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Buildings (freehold structure) | 40 years | Nil |
| Building services and plant | 15 years | Nil |
| Leasehold improvements | Shorter of lease term or 15 years | Nil |
| Production plant and machinery | 10 years | Nil |
| Motor vehicles | 5 years | 15 per cent of cost |
| Furniture and fittings | 7 years | Nil |
| IT hardware | 3 years | Nil |

Specifically, **buildings are depreciated over 40 years** and **IT hardware is depreciated over 3
years**. Freehold land is not depreciated.

Useful lives and residual values are reviewed annually at the interim close. A change is a change
in estimate and is applied prospectively; it is never applied by restating prior periods.

## 4. Componentisation

Where an asset comprises parts with materially different useful lives, each significant part is
depreciated separately. In practice this applies to buildings — structure, roof, services and
fit-out are separated at acquisition — and to production lines with a short-life tooling element.

## 5. Assets under construction

Costs are accumulated in assets under construction and are not depreciated. The asset is
transferred into its class and depreciation begins when it is capable of operating in the manner
intended by management, which is not necessarily the date it is first used commercially. Projects
open in assets under construction for more than twelve months are reviewed each quarter for
impairment indicators under PG-0030.

## 6. Disposal and write-off

On disposal, cost and accumulated depreciation are removed and the gain or loss is recognised in
other operating income or expense. Gains are never recognised in revenue.

Any disposal or write-off of an asset with a **net book value above USD 50,000** requires Finance
Director approval before the asset is removed from the register, with evidence of the disposal
route and any proceeds. Below that amount, Department Head approval is sufficient. Assets that
cannot be located during a verification count are written off only after a documented search, and
a pattern of unlocatable assets is reported to Internal Audit.

## 7. Physical verification

Every entity performs a physical verification of its asset register at least every two years,
covering all assets with a net book value over USD 25,000 and a sample of the remainder.
Differences are investigated before the register is adjusted, not after.

## 8. Fully depreciated assets

Assets that remain in use are retained in the register at nil net book value. They are not written
back, revalued, or given a fresh useful life.
