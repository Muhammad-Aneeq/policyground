# Revenue Recognition — Core Principles

This policy summarises the Group's approach to recognising revenue. It is written in the Group's
own words as a working summary for finance teams. It is not a substitute for the accounting
standards themselves, and where a transaction is unusual, a written technical position is obtained
under PG-0001 §6 before revenue is recognised.

## 1. The core question

Revenue is recognised when the Group transfers to a customer the goods or services it promised, in
an amount reflecting what the Group expects to be entitled to in exchange. Two ideas do most of the
work: *transfer of control* determines **when**, and *entitlement* determines **how much**.

Neither cash receipt nor invoicing determines recognition. Cash received before control transfers
is a contract liability. Control transferred before invoicing gives rise to a contract asset.

## 2. The five questions the Group asks

For every arrangement, in order:

1. **Is there a contract?** An enforceable agreement with commercial substance, approved by both
   parties, with identifiable rights, payment terms, and probable collection.
2. **What did we promise?** The distinct goods or services, identified individually.
3. **What are we entitled to?** The transaction price, including variable amounts and any non-cash
   consideration.
4. **How does the price attach to the promises?** Allocation across the promises identified in
   step 2.
5. **When does each promise get satisfied?** At a point in time, or over time.

Steps 2, 3 and 4 are elaborated in PG-0012, PG-0013 and PG-0012 respectively.

## 3. Contract existence and collectability

If collection is not probable at inception, no contract exists for accounting purposes and no
revenue is recognised, even where goods have shipped. Consideration received in such a case is held
as a liability until either collection becomes probable, the contract is terminated, or the
consideration is non-refundable and the Group has stopped transferring goods or services.

Collectability is assessed on the customer's ability and intention to pay, not on the Group's
willingness to pursue. A deteriorating credit position after inception is a receivables question
under PG-0028, not a reason to reverse revenue already recognised.

## 4. Combining contracts

Two or more contracts with the same customer, entered into at or near the same time, are accounted
for as one where they are negotiated as a package with a single commercial objective, where the
consideration in one depends on the other, or where the goods promised form a single performance
obligation. Sales teams structuring an arrangement across multiple documents does not by itself
create separate accounting contracts.

## 5. Point in time or over time

Revenue is recognised over time where one of the following holds:

- the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefit as the Group performs — typical of
  routine service and subscription arrangements;
- the Group's performance creates or enhances an asset the customer controls as it is created;
- the Group's performance creates an asset with no alternative use to the Group, and the Group has
  an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date, including a reasonable margin.

Otherwise revenue is recognised at the point control transfers. Indicators of transfer include the
customer having a present obligation to pay, legal title, physical possession, the significant
risks and rewards of ownership, and acceptance.

Where revenue is recognised over time, progress is measured by an output method wherever a reliable
output measure exists. Input methods such as cost-to-cost are used only where outputs cannot be
measured reliably, and the basis chosen is applied consistently to similar contracts.

## 6. Principal or agent

Where a third party is involved in providing goods or services, the Group determines whether it
controls the good or service before transfer. If it does, it is a principal and reports revenue
gross. If it does not, it is an agent and reports the net commission. Indicators of control include
primary responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk, and discretion in setting the price.

The distinction changes reported revenue dramatically without changing profit, which is precisely
why it is assessed by Group Technical Accounting and not locally.

## 7. Bill-and-hold

Revenue on goods billed but not shipped is recognised only where the arrangement is substantive and
requested by the customer, the goods are separately identified as belonging to the customer, they
are ready for physical transfer, and the Group cannot direct them to another customer. All four
conditions are documented per arrangement.

## 8. Presentation

Revenue is presented net of trade discounts, rebates and sales taxes collected on behalf of a
government. Amounts billed to customers for shipping are revenue where shipping is not a separate
promise. Shipping and handling costs incurred by the Group are cost of sales, never a reduction of
revenue.
