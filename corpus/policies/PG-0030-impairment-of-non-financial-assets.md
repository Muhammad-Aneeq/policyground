# Impairment of Non-Financial Assets

This policy covers when an impairment test is required, how the test is performed, and how the
result is recorded. It applies to property, plant and equipment, right-of-use assets, intangible
assets, goodwill and contract cost assets.

## 1. When a test is required

Annually, regardless of indicators, for: goodwill, intangible assets not yet available for use, and
intangible assets with an indefinite useful life.

For everything else, whenever an indicator of impairment exists. Each entity assesses indicators at
every reporting date.

## 2. Indicators

**External:** a significant decline in market value beyond the effect of normal use; a significant
adverse change in the technological, market, economic or legal environment; an increase in interest
rates affecting the discount rate; the Group's net assets exceeding its market capitalisation.

**Internal:** physical damage or obsolescence; a decision to discontinue or restructure the
operation, including a plan recognised under PG-0024; evidence that economic performance is or will
be worse than expected; a change in the manner or extent of an asset's use.

The Group applies a standing quantitative trigger alongside the qualitative list. Where a
cash-generating unit **underperforms its approved plan by more than 15 per cent** on operating profit
for two consecutive quarters, an impairment test is performed whether or not any other indicator is
present. The trigger is a floor, not a ceiling: a test is still performed where judgement says one is
warranted at a smaller variance.

## 3. Cash-generating units

Assets are tested individually where they generate largely independent cash inflows. Where they do
not, they are tested as part of the smallest cash-generating unit to which they belong.

Units are defined by Group Technical Accounting and are not redefined by entities. The definition
changes only where the way the business is managed and monitored has changed, and a change is
disclosed with the reason.

Goodwill is allocated to the units expected to benefit from the acquisition. Allocation is completed
within twelve months of the acquisition date and is not revisited to avoid an impairment.

## 4. Recoverable amount

The higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use.

Where fair value less costs of disposal alone exceeds the carrying amount, the test stops there and
value in use need not be calculated. This is worth checking first for property, where a market
valuation is often readily obtainable.

**Value in use** is the present value of the future cash flows expected from the unit in its current
condition:

- Cash flow projections are based on the most recent budget and plan approved by the Board, covering
  no more than five years. A longer explicit period requires Group Technical Accounting approval and
  evidence that longer-range forecasts have been reliable.
- Beyond the explicit period, a steady or declining growth rate is used, not exceeding the long-term
  average growth rate for the market, and the rate used is disclosed.
- Projections exclude cash flows from a future restructuring not yet committed, and from enhancing
  the asset's performance beyond its current condition. Both are common errors and both flatter the
  result.
- The discount rate is pre-tax and reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and
  the risks specific to the unit. Rates are provided by Group Treasury by unit and are not derived
  locally.

## 5. Recognising a loss

An impairment loss reduces goodwill allocated to the unit first, then the other assets of the unit
pro rata on their carrying amounts. No individual asset is reduced below the highest of its fair
value less costs of disposal, its value in use, and zero.

Losses on assets carried at cost are recognised in profit or loss immediately. Any loss above the
**group performance materiality of USD 1,500,000** is reported to the Finance Director before it is
posted, so that the disclosure and the external explanation are prepared alongside the entry rather
than after it.

## 6. Reversal

Impairment losses on assets other than goodwill are reversed where the estimates used to determine
recoverable amount have changed, but only up to the carrying amount that would have been determined
had no impairment been recognised, net of the depreciation that would have been charged.

**Impairment of goodwill is never reversed.** A reversal is recognised in the period the change in
estimate occurs and is explained in the flux commentary under PG-0016.

## 7. Documentation

Every test performed produces a memorandum recording: the unit tested, the trigger, the method, the
key assumptions with their source, the sensitivity of the conclusion to each key assumption, the
result, and the approver. Memoranda for tests concluding no impairment are retained on the same basis
as those concluding a loss — the reasoning matters equally, and a test that concluded "no impairment"
without a retained memorandum will be reperformed.
