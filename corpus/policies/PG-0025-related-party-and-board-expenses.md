# Related-Party Transactions and Board Expense Protocol

**Distribution:** Chief Financial Officer, Company Secretary, Group Counsel, Chair of the Audit
Committee, and the two named members of the Group Reporting team who prepare the related-party
disclosure. Entity finance teams are not given access; where an entity posts a transaction caught by
this policy, it is routed centrally.

## 1. Why this policy is restricted

The register maintained under this policy contains directors' personal interests, family
relationships and private financial connections. It is personal data with a narrow lawful basis for
processing, and the register is held accordingly.

## 2. Who is a related party

- Members of the Board and the Executive Committee, and their close family members.
- Entities controlled or jointly controlled by any of the above, or over which they have significant
  influence.
- Joint ventures, associates and post-employment benefit plans of the Group.
- The parent, subsidiaries and fellow subsidiaries, for entity-level statutory reporting.

Close family members include a spouse or domestic partner, their children and dependants, and the
children and dependants of the individual. The test is influence in substance, so a person outside
that list who could be expected to influence, or be influenced by, the individual is included where
the facts support it.

## 3. The register

The Company Secretary maintains a register of declared interests, refreshed at least annually and
within five working days of any change. Each entry records the individual, the nature of the
interest, the counterparty, the date declared, and whether any Group transaction exists with that
counterparty.

The register is held in the restricted governance workspace as **Ledger Annexe Q-Twelve**, with
access logged and reviewed by the Audit Committee each half year.

Failure to declare is treated as a governance breach and is reported to the Chair of the Audit
Committee regardless of the value involved.

## 4. Approval of related-party transactions

Every transaction with a related party requires Chief Financial Officer and Audit Committee approval
before it is entered into, whatever its value, per PG-0007 §4. The interested party takes no part in
the decision, receives no papers relating to it, and is not present for the discussion.

Approval requires evidence that the terms are no more favourable than would be available from an
unconnected party, obtained independently of the related party. A quotation supplied by the related
party is not independent evidence.

## 5. Recording

Transactions caught by this policy are flagged in the ledger with the related-party indicator at the
point of posting, not identified retrospectively by searching for names before the year end.
Retrospective identification has repeatedly proven unreliable and is not an acceptable process.

Balances with related parties are reconciled and confirmed at each half year and are never netted
against unrelated balances.

## 6. Board and Executive expenses

Expenses of Board members and the Executive Committee follow PG-0006 in full. The following
additional controls apply:

| Control | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Approval of an Executive Committee member's expenses | Chief Financial Officer |
| Approval of the Chief Financial Officer's expenses | Chief Executive Officer |
| Approval of the Chief Executive Officer's expenses | Chair of the Audit Committee |
| Approval of a Non-Executive Director's expenses | Company Secretary |
| Use of chartered or private aviation | Chief Executive Officer in advance, with the reason recorded |
| Spouse or partner travel | Chair of the Audit Committee in advance; treated as a taxable benefit |

No member of the Board or Executive Committee approves their own expenses in any circumstance,
including where the approver is unavailable. Where an approver is absent, the claim waits.

## 7. Annual review and disclosure

The Audit Committee reviews, at least annually: the register, every related-party transaction
entered into during the year with its approval evidence, and a sample of Board and Executive expense
claims selected by Internal Audit.

The related-party disclosure in the financial statements is prepared by the two named Group
Reporting preparers from the register and the ledger flag, reconciled between the two sources, and
approved by the Company Secretary and the Chief Financial Officer before it is issued.

## 8. Breach

A transaction entered into without the approval required by §4 is reported to the Audit Committee
immediately on discovery, is unwound where that is possible, and is disclosed whether or not it is
unwound. The disclosure obligation does not depend on the Group's ability to reverse the
transaction.
