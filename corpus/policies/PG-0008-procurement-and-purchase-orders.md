# Procurement, Purchase Orders and Supplier Onboarding

No purchase order, no payment. This policy sets out how the Group commits to a supplier and how
that commitment is recorded before the work happens rather than after the invoice arrives.

## 1. Purchase order requirement

A **purchase order is required for any commitment above USD 2,500**. The purchase order is raised
and approved *before* the supplier is instructed. A purchase order raised after the invoice has
been received is a retrospective order; it is recorded as such, requires approval one band above
the value under PG-0007, and is reported monthly to the Financial Controller.

The following categories are exempt from the purchase order requirement because they are controlled
by another mechanism: payroll and payroll taxes; rent and service charges under a signed lease;
utilities under a Group contract; statutory fees; intercompany recharges; and employee expense
claims under PG-0006.

## 2. Sourcing thresholds

| Commitment value | Sourcing requirement |
|---|---|
| Up to USD 25,000 | One written quotation |
| USD 25,001 to USD 100,000 | Three written quotations, or a documented sole-source justification |
| USD 100,001 to USD 500,000 | Formal request for proposal, evaluation matrix retained |
| Above USD 500,000 | Formal tender with Procurement leading and Finance Director on the panel |

Sole-source justification is a written case explaining why alternatives cannot meet the
requirement. "The incumbent knows our systems" is a consideration, not a justification, and it is
never sufficient on its own above USD 100,000.

## 3. Approval

Purchase orders route through the matrix in PG-0007. A **Cost Centre Manager may approve
commitments up to USD 5,000**; above that, the order routes to the appropriate band. The approver
of a purchase order may not be the person who raised the requisition.

Framework agreements are approved once, at the value of the maximum committed spend over the
agreement's life. Call-offs within an approved framework do not need re-approval at band level, but
each call-off still requires a purchase order.

## 4. Supplier onboarding

A supplier is created in the master file only after Procurement has obtained and verified:

- legal name, registered address and company registration number;
- tax registration and, where applicable, withholding tax status;
- bank details confirmed by a call-back to a number obtained independently of the supplier's
  own correspondence — never a number given on the invoice or in the email requesting the change;
- sanctions and adverse-media screening;
- confirmation that no employee or director has an undisclosed interest in the supplier.

Onboarding takes ten working days for a standard supplier. Urgency does not shorten the bank
verification step; it may shorten only the commercial review.

## 5. Bank detail changes

A change to a supplier's bank details is treated as a new onboarding for verification purposes. It
requires a call-back to a previously known contact, is made by a person who cannot approve
payments, and triggers a hold on the next payment run to that supplier for manual confirmation.
This is the Group's principal control against payment redirection fraud, and there is no
fast-track.

## 6. Contract terms

The Group standard is **standard payment terms of Net 45** from receipt of a valid invoice. Terms
shorter than Net 30 require Treasurer approval. Early-settlement discounts are taken where the
implied annual rate exceeds the Group cost of funds; Treasury publishes the current comparison
rate each quarter.

Purchase orders are raised for the value agreed. Where an invoice exceeds the purchase order, the
**three-way match tolerance of 2 per cent** of the order value **or USD 50, whichever is lower**
applies. Anything beyond tolerance requires an amended purchase order approved at the band
appropriate to the revised total, not to the increment.

## 7. Goods receipt

Receipt is confirmed by the person who received the goods or accepted the service, not by the
person who raised the order and not by Accounts Payable. For services, receipt means the work was
performed, evidenced by a deliverable, a timesheet, or a signed acceptance.

Open purchase orders with no receipt after ninety days are reviewed with the requisitioner and
closed if the commitment has lapsed, so that the commitment ledger reflects reality at the close.

## 8. Prohibited practices

Engaging a supplier before the order is approved; splitting a requirement to avoid a sourcing
threshold; accepting a gift or hospitality from a bidder during an active tender; and disclosing
one bidder's pricing to another. Each is reported to the Group Compliance Officer.
