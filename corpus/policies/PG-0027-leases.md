# Leases — Recognition, Measurement and Remeasurement

This policy covers lessee accounting for the Group. Property leases dominate the portfolio, and the
judgements that matter most are the lease term and the discount rate.

## 1. Is there a lease?

A contract contains a lease where it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for
a period in exchange for consideration. Control requires both the right to obtain substantially all
the economic benefit from use, and the right to direct how and for what purpose the asset is used.

Where the supplier has a substantive right to substitute the asset, there is no identified asset and
no lease. A substitution right is substantive only where the supplier has the practical ability to
substitute and would benefit economically from doing so.

Service arrangements are assessed at inception, not assumed. Managed print, data centre capacity and
vehicle-with-driver arrangements are the recurring cases where the answer is not obvious.

## 2. Exemptions

The Group applies both available exemptions and, where applied, recognises the payments as an
expense on a straight-line basis over the term:

- **Short-term leases** — a term of twelve months or less at commencement, with no purchase option.
  Applied by class of underlying asset.
- **Low-value leases** — the **low-value lease threshold of USD 5,000** is applied to the value of
  the underlying asset when new, not to the value of the lease payments and not to the asset's
  current condition. Applied lease by lease.

A lease is not short-term merely because the remaining term has fallen below twelve months; the test
is at commencement. Where an exemption is taken, the fact and the amount are disclosed.

## 3. Initial measurement

The lease liability is the present value of the unpaid lease payments, comprising fixed payments,
in-substance fixed payments, variable payments that depend on an index or rate measured at the
commencement rate, expected residual value guarantee payments, the exercise price of a purchase
option reasonably certain to be exercised, and termination penalties where the term reflects
termination.

Variable payments linked to usage or turnover are excluded and expensed as incurred.

The right-of-use asset is the liability, plus payments made at or before commencement, plus initial
direct costs, plus the estimated cost of dismantling or restoring, less any lease incentive
received.

## 4. Discount rate

The rate implicit in the lease is used where it can be readily determined, which for property leases
it generally cannot. Otherwise the incremental borrowing rate is used: the rate the entity would pay
to borrow, over a similar term, with similar security, to obtain an asset of similar value in a
similar economic environment.

Group Treasury publishes a rate table each quarter by currency, entity credit profile and lease
term. Entities use the published rate and do not derive their own. Using the Group's own cost of
funds for a small subsidiary in a different currency is a common error and is not permitted.

## 5. Lease term

The non-cancellable period, plus optional extension periods where extension is reasonably certain,
plus periods after an optional termination date where termination is reasonably certain not to
occur.

"Reasonably certain" is a high threshold and is assessed on economic incentive, not on intention:
the cost of leasehold improvements that would be abandoned, relocation cost, the importance of the
location to operations, and whether the rent is below market. The assessment is documented at
commencement and revisited only when a significant event within the Group's control changes it.

## 6. Subsequent measurement

The liability is increased by interest and reduced by payments. The right-of-use asset is
depreciated on a straight-line basis over the shorter of the lease term and the useful life of the
asset, except where ownership transfers or a purchase option is reasonably certain, in which case the
useful life is used.

Right-of-use assets are included in the impairment assessment under PG-0030 on the same basis as
owned assets.

## 7. Remeasurement and modification

| Event | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Change in an index or rate affecting future payments | Remeasure liability, unchanged discount rate, adjust the asset |
| Change in the assessment of an extension or termination option | Remeasure liability, revised discount rate, adjust the asset |
| Change in expected residual value guarantee payment | Remeasure liability, unchanged discount rate, adjust the asset |
| Increase in scope at a price commensurate with standalone price | Separate new lease |
| Increase in scope not at standalone price, or decrease in scope | Modification: remeasure with a revised discount rate; a decrease also reduces the asset with a gain or loss |
| Rent concession granted by the lessor | Assess as a modification unless a practical expedient applies |

Where remeasurement reduces the asset below zero, the excess is recognised in profit or loss.

## 8. Presentation and data

Right-of-use assets are presented separately from owned property, plant and equipment. Interest is a
finance cost and the principal element of payments is a financing cash flow.

Every lease is recorded in the Group lease system at commencement, with the contract attached. A
lease known to finance only when the first payment appears in the bank has already failed this
control, and the gap is reported to the Financial Controller.
